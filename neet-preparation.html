01 · THE PREPARATION SYSTEM

Preparation Is a Cycle — Not a One-Time Reading

A chapter becomes useful for NEET only when you can understand it, solve from it, recall it later and apply it under time pressure.

Core Principle Do not measure preparation only by “How many chapters did I finish?” Measure it by “How many chapters can I still understand, recall and solve from correctly?”
02 · THREE SUBJECTS · THREE METHODS

Physics, Chemistry and Biology Should Not Be Studied the Same Way

The goal is common — accuracy in NEET — but the learning process is different for each subject.

P
PHYSICS

Understand → Apply

1. Concept firstKnow what the quantity, law or relation actually means.

2. Formula meaningUnderstand variables, units, conditions and direction — not just the equation.

3. Basic numericalsStart with direct application before mixed or tricky questions.

4. NEET-level practiceBuild speed only after the method is clear.

5. Error reviewSeparate concept errors from calculation and interpretation errors.

PHYSICAL

Concepts → Formula logic → Numericals → Mixed practice

INORGANIC

ORGANIC

Basic concepts → Mechanism thinking → Reactions → Connections → Questions

THREE BRANCHES · THREE APPROACHES
B
BIOLOGY

Read → Recall → Revise

1. Careful readingRead line by line with attention to keywords, examples and exceptions.

2. Diagrams & labelsUse visual memory where the chapter depends on structure or process.

3. Active recallClose the book and retrieve what you learned.

4. MCQsUse questions to expose missed statements and confusing options.

5. Repeated revisionBiology strength comes from retention, not one-time reading.

07 · QUESTION PRACTICE & ERROR ANALYSIS

Questions Are Not Just for Marks — They Diagnose Learning

The number of questions matters less than whether you understand why each mistake happened.

EASY / DIRECT→CONCEPT→APPLICATION→MIXED→PYQ→TIMED
01CONCEPT ERROR

You did not understand the underlying idea clearly enough.

02MEMORY ERROR

You knew it earlier, but could not recall the required fact or relation.

03CALCULATION ERROR

The method was right, but arithmetic, units or signs failed.

04READING ERROR

You missed a keyword, condition, NOT/incorrect statement or option detail.

05SILLY ERROR

You knew the answer but marked, copied or interpreted carelessly.

06TIME-PRESSURE ERROR

You rushed, guessed poorly or got trapped because of time management.

After every test, ask: “What type of mistake cost this mark — and what specific change will prevent the same mistake next time?”

09 · BACKLOG RECOVERY

Falling Behind Does Not Mean Starting Everything Again

Recover in a controlled way while continuing present learning.

01STOP PANIC

Do not randomly jump between many incomplete chapters.

02LIST THE BACKLOG

Write exactly what is incomplete — units, topics, questions or revision.

03FIND FOUNDATIONS

Recover prerequisite chapters before dependent chapters.

04CREATE SMALL BLOCKS

Give backlog a fixed daily/weekly recovery block.

05CONTINUE CURRENT WORK

Do not allow recovery to create a new backlog.

THE PREPARATION COMPASS

Before You Move On, Ask Yourself Five Questions

Do I understand it? Can I solve from it? Can I recall it? Can I do it under time? Can I still do it after a week?

When the answer becomes “yes” repeatedly, preparation is becoming exam-ready.
